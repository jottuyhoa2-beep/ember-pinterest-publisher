Jelly blush: how to get the glossy look

Jelly blush looks nothing like powder blush, and it does not act like it either. It is a bouncy gel stick that goes on wet, feels cool on the skin and dries down to a glossy tint. The finish is the point: cheeks that look lit from inside, not dusted on top.

1. What jelly texture is

Jelly is a gel, not a cream and not a powder. It is firm in the stick and soft on the skin. It melts a little with the warmth of your fingers. The colour is sheer, so it builds instead of landing all at once. Milk Makeup's Cooling Water Jelly Tint is our pick: a cooling gel stick in Splash, a berry shade that suits most skin tones.

2. How to apply it

Swipe once across the apple of your cheek, then blend with your fingers right away. Do not wait. Jelly sets fast, and once it sets it stays put. Use your fingertips, not a brush, and press the colour in with a tapping motion. The same stick works on lips: one swipe, press your lips together, done.

3. One swipe or two

One swipe is a light wash, good for daytime, work, or a no-makeup look. Two swipes is a proper berry, good for evenings or when the rest of your face is simple. Build it one swipe at a time and look in the mirror between swipes. It is easy to add more. It is hard to take it off.

4. What pairs with it

Jelly blush pairs best with a simple face. Moisturised skin first, then the blush, then nothing heavy on top. A little on the bridge of the nose gives a sun-kissed look, but keep it light. It also pairs well with a vanilla scent stack: glossy cheeks and warm vanilla smell like the same afternoon.

5. The mistakes to avoid

Two mistakes ruin jelly blush. Putting it over powder, which makes it pill and clump, and waiting too long to blend, which leaves a stripe. Always put jelly on bare, moisturised skin, and blend the second it touches your cheek. Too much at once is the third mistake, and the fix is the same: one swipe at a time.
