How to choose between the two makers

Both make a pot that lasts for years if she treats it right. The differences that matter are small. A dark interior hides stains from searing meat. A light interior shows how brown the onions are. A self basting lid keeps a slow stew moist. A flat lid doubles as a baking sheet. If she sears meat often, pick a pot with a dark interior. If she bakes bread, pick the one with the heaviest lid she can lift with one hand. Colour is the last choice, not the first. Red and orange read festive on a Christmas table. Blue and green sit quietly in a kitchen all year. Cream shows every mark, so it suits a careful cook.

Whichever maker you pick, buy the size she will use weekly, not the size that looks grand on a shelf. A pot that is too big stays in the cupboard. A pot that fits her stove and her oven gets used.
